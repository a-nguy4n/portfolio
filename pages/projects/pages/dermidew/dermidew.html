Overview

DermiDew at a Glance

Overview Summary

Skincare, Smarter

DermiDew is a smart vanity mirror and web app that analyzes users’ skin to provide personalized routines, ingredient guidance, and product recommendations based on their skincare needs.

Problem

Personalized skincare is often inaccessible

Users often spend significant time and money trying products without knowing what will work for their skin. Existing skin-analysis apps provide limited guidance, while more advanced analyzers remain too expensive for the everyday consumer.

Solution

Addressing the Accessibility Gap

DermiDew combines a camera-equipped vanity mirror with a web app to make personalized skincare more accessible. The mirror captures and analyzes the user’s skin, while the web app turns those results into personalized routines, ingredient guidance, and product recommendations.

Research & Discovery

Is There a Real Need?

We started with what we thought users needed. Then, we asked them.

To ensure our idea addressed a real need, we spoke with potential users to validate the problem, identify our target audience, and understand their skincare habits, challenges, and preferences. Our findings helped shape the direction of our solution.

Research at a Glance

32

User Interviews

We interviewed 32 potential users from a range of backgrounds, including beauty store employees, teens, parents, and college students. We explored their skincare routines, concerns, and how they choose and purchase products.

From Conversations to Concerns

01

“Where do I even start?”

With so many products, brands, and ingredients to choose from, many people felt overwhelmed and unsure of what was actually right for their skin.

02

“How do I know what works?”

People were tired of spending time and money trying products without knowing whether they were actually helping their skin or addressing their concerns.

03

“What should my routine look like?”

Many people knew what products they owned, but not always why they were using them, which ones worked together, or what order they should apply them in.

Product Strategy

Shaping the Product

The product we ended with wasn't the product we started with.

Our initial idea evolved into a product informed by the needs, challenges, and behaviors we uncovered through our interviews. From there, we focused on turning the concept into a tangible product by defining our MVP, validating the business opportunity, and using our findings to guide its future direction.

Our Product Evolution

The Idea Stayed. The Form Changed.

  1. Original Concept

    Face Mask

    A wearable concept that used an embedded camera to capture and analyze the user’s skin.

    • Limited camera distance
    • Poor image quality
    • Reluctance to wear a facial device
    • Difficult to use in daily routines

  2. First Pivot

    Wand

    A handheld device that gave users more control over positioning the camera and capturing different areas of their skin.

    • Image quality remained inconsistent
    • Required multiple images to capture full face
    • Added a separate step to the skincare routine

  3. Final Direction

    Vanity Mirror

    A stationary setup that created the distance needed for consistent image capture while bringing skin analysis into a familiar part of getting ready.

    • More consistent image quality
    • Captured the full face in a single image
    • Integrated well into the getting-ready routine

Why the Mirror?

Rather than adding an extra step, we designed DermiDew around the way people already get ready.

The mirror gave our camera the distance needed to capture the full face in a single, clearer image. This made our scans faster and much more consistent. The mirror also placed DermiDew in a familiar part of many skincare and getting-ready routines, looking in the mirror. As such, having our product integrated with a mirror would make it feel like a natural addition rather than a separate task.

Defining the MVP

Connecting Setup to Results

We focused our MVP on three key steps, making it easy to get started, capturing a clear face scan, and giving users results they could quickly understand and use.

  1. 1 Capture Scan Your Face

    The mirror’s built-in camera captures a full-face image without requiring users to hold or position a separate device. The fixed setup creates a more consistent distance and angle. This improves image quality while keeping the scanning process quick and hands-free.

    • Consistent capture
    • Full-face scan
  2. 2 Analyze Understand Your Skin

    The captured image is sent for analysis to identify visible skin characteristics and key concerns. DermiDew organizes the results into a clear summary so users can easily see what areas of their skin may need attention.

    • Skin analysis
    • Hydration
    • Key concerns
  3. 3 Personalize Know What to Do

    DermiDew turns the analysis into personalized recommendations users can act on. The web app provides morning and night routines, explains key product ingredients, recommends products based on identified concerns, and gives users a structured way to follow their skincare routine.

    • Routine outline
    • Product recommendations

Interface Evolution

Elevating the Our Web App

Our first version focused on getting the main features to work. After testing it, we revisited the layout, reorganized the content, and cleaned up the visuals to make the app easier to use.

Physical Product

We developed two mirror prototypes to explore how different sizes and setups could accommodate the camera while fitting into users' skincare routines. The smaller handheld mirror became our main focus for the MVP, while the standing vanity mirror allowed us to explore a larger design.

Development

Technicalities of DermiDew

Building DermiDew required combining hardware engineering, software development, and AI into one functional system. We explored different approaches and adapted to the needs of the project, tackling technical challenges.

System Architecture

How DermiDew Works

    Requests a scan

  2. 02 ESP32-CAM

    Captures the image

  3. 03 AI API

  4. 04 Web App

Technical Challenges

What We Had to Work Through

01

API Integration

Connecting to an external AI service required secure credential management and reliable communication between the backend and API.

Our Approach

We used environment variables to keep sensitive API credentials outside the codebase.

02

Dynamic Interface

We needed the website to update dynamically without relying on a frontend framework.

Our Approach

We used JavaScript fetch() to load modular HTML components and update the interface without reloading the entire page.

Iteration

Adapting to Technical Constraints

Our early prototypes faced challenges with camera distance, image quality, and capturing the full face. The face mask required complicated image stitching, while the wand still produced inconsistent results and required too much manual positioning. We chose the mirror because it allowed for better image capture, a simpler setup, and a more natural fit into users' daily routines.

Reflection

Lessons & Next Steps

Learning to Design with an Open Mind

DermiDew taught me to challenge assumptions, embrace feedback, and balance user needs with technical and business constraints. I learned that great product design isn't about getting everything right the first time, but knowing when to rethink, simplify, and improve an idea.

Looking Forward

Given more time, I would test whether users consistently follow their routines, trust the recommendations, and find value in tracking their progress. I would also improve the connection between the mirror and website, while exploring a more accessible app only experience.
