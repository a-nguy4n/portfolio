Overview

At long last, after going through cycles of iterations and scrapped ideas, I have finally been able to push out my portfolio that is authentic to me. I believe that a portfolio is not just a showcase of my own work, but also a digital documentation of my own growth and my own little corner of the Internet.

I decided to design and build my portfolio from scratch because I really wanted to challenge myself creatively and technically. Rather than relying on pre-made templates, I wanted the freedom to experiment and explore my own creative direction , all while learning how to translate my ideas into a fully functional website. And in a way, it has become its own project that has led me to look inward, reflect on who I am, and discover how I want to represent myself to the world.

My Vision & Goals

THE VISION

Building My Own Little Corner of the Internet

When I first began planning my portfolio, I found myself caught between what a portfolio was expected to look like and what I actually wanted mine to be. Browsing through countless sleek, minimalist designs left me wondering whether I should follow the same direction or explore something more personal. But beyond finding the right visual direction, I also had to figure out which parts of my personality, interests, and experiences I wanted my portfolio to reflect.

THE CREATIVE CHALLENGE

How might I translate my personality and creative vision into an interactive portfolio that prioritizes authenticity over trends, while balancing creative expression, technical experimentation, and usability?

My Design Process

1. Finding Inspiration & Exploring Ideas

I began my design process by searching for inspiration, but I didn't want to limit myself to looking at other portfolios. I wanted to explore inspiration from different perspectives. I looked inward at my own experiences and the things that felt personal to me. I also explored how others expressed their creativity through art and design. Along the way, I collected unexpected ideas that caught my eye. Through this process, I hoped to find common themes in the things I loved and weave them into my own design.

Takeaway

TEAM'S KEY INSIGHT

The problem isn't a lack of events. It's a lack of connection.

Building SoCalSocial taught my team and I that creating a product involves more than designing interfaces and implementing features. We had to consider the needs of both students and organizations while working within our technical abilities and project timeline.


Throughout the process, we learned to prioritize essential features, recognize potential risks early, and adapt when our initial ideas became too complex. Communication and collaboration helped us turn different ideas into one cohesive product.

MY LESSONS LEARNED

This project strengthened my skills in team management, collaboration, and problem-solving. I learned to coordinate responsibilities based on individual strengths, communicate design decisions, and keep the team aligned on a shared vision. Working across design and development challenged me to consider how design decisions affect implementation and the overall user experience.


Revisiting SoCalSocial independently gave me a new perspective on my original work. I became more intentional about information hierarchy, responsive design, and designing for different types of users.


Above all, I learned the importance of continuous evaluation, clear communication, and being willing to revisit earlier decisions.
