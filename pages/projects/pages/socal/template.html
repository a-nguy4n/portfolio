Overview

SoCalSocial is a campus event discovery and organization platform designed for UC San Diego students. The platform brings events from student organizations into one centralized space, making it easier for students to discover activities, save events they’re interested in, and keep track of what’s happening on campus.


My team and I designed and developed SoCalSocial with both students and organizations in mind, creating an experience that simplifies event discovery for students while giving organizations a dedicated space to publish and manage their events.

Problem

THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Campus events are everywhere.
Finding them shouldn't be.

Campus events are shared across social media, flyers, club tabling, and word of mouth, making it difficult for students to discover what's happening and keep track of events they're interested in.

THE CHALLENGE

How might we create one centralized space for students to easily discover and organize campus events while giving student organizations a reliable way to publish and manage them?

Research

I used two research methods to understand both sides of the campus event landscape: how students discover and keep track of events and how student organizations promote events and reach their audiences.

User Interviews

I spoke with both students and members of student organizations to understand their experiences firsthand.

  • Students: How they discover events, keep track of them, and which platforms they already rely on.
  • Organizations: How they promote events, reach students, and manage information across different channels.

Desk Research

I examined the existing campus event landscape to understand where and how event information was being shared.

  • Students: Looked at the platforms and channels students encounter when searching for campus activities.
  • Organizations: Investigated how organizations use social media, messaging platforms, and other campus channels to promote their events.

STUDENT

Key Insights

  • Students discover campus events across multiple platforms and channels.
  • Finding the most relevant event details is not always quick or straightforward.
  • Students need an easier way to keep track of events they're interested in.

ORGANIZATION

Key Insights

  • Organizations often promote the same event across multiple platforms.
  • Organizations can struggle to reach students outside their existing audience.
  • Organizations need a central place to publish and maintain event information.
OUR DIRECTION

One shared space for both sides.

These findings helped lead my team and I to create a shared platform where students could find and organize events, and organizations could post and manage them. With that direction established, we began defining the core experience needed to support both sides.

DEFINING THE MVP

With many potential features to consider, I helped to narrow our focus to four core features that addressed the key needs of students and organizations.

Event Discovery

Bring events into one browsable experience where students can discover what’s happening across campus while giving organizations a centralized space to share their events and reach new audiences.

Browse Search Organizations

Accounts

Give students and organizations their own accounts so students can save and manage events, while organizations can publish and manage the events they host.

Design

Mapping the
Campus Experience

To begin developing SoCal Social, I started by creating sketches and wireframes to visualize the initial concept and establish a foundation for the design. Along the way, I identified potential risks, worked through technical challenges, and made design decisions based on user needs and what my team and I could build within our given timeframe.

1.

FIRST DRAFT

Turning Requirements into Wireframes

I began by translating our team's MVP features into the essential screens students and organizations would need to complete their core tasks.

I created low-fidelity sketches to explore how students could discover events and organizations, as well as how information would be organized across the platform. Through this process, I was able to quickly visualize ideas in collaboration with my team. Also, I was able to consider how the user experience would differ between students and organizations before moving into more detailed wireframes.

WHAT I LEARNED

I realized that events should be the central focus of the platform, connecting organizations that publish events with students who discover and manage them.

2.

SIMPLIFYING THE DESIGN

Prioritizing What Matters

As I improve our team designs, I had take in consideration the technical challenges with implementing our feautures. For instance, features like calendars, social interactions, and account permissions added complexity. After talking to my teammates, I then explored simpler alternatives that still addressed our users' needs, such as pivoting from a full calendar to an organized event list that is ordered by date and time.

    Design Decisions and Shifts

  • Full calendar → Organized event list
  • Broad social features → Event discovery
  • Complex permissions → Simpler accounts
  • Multiple interactions → Essential event actions

These design decisions helped us maintain a focused user experience while minimizing potential development challenges that could delay our progress.

3.

BUILDING IT

Planning Around Technical Challenges

Before development, my team and I had identified potential risks involving authentication, scalability, and code complexity. I evaluated how these challenges could affect both the user experience and our team's ability to develop the platform within the given timeline. Rather than attempting to implement every feature, I worked with my team to develop backup plans, such as simplifying account permissions or narrowing the scope from broad social features to essential event discovery functionality.

Additionally. I helped plan our technology stack around the team's existing experience, selecting React, TypeScript, Flask, and SQL. We divided responsibilities based on individual strengths, prioritize essential features, and establish realistic limits for early users. These decisions helped us minimize development risks, maintain a manageable scope, and leave room for future improvements.

React TypeScript HTML / CSS Python Flask SQL
4.

REFLECTING & REDESIGNING

Revisiting the Experience

After building SoCalSocial, I revisited the original designs to explore how I could improve the overall experience. I focused on making navigation more detailed and smooth, improving how event and organization information was presented, and adapting the interface for mobile devices.

For the Explore page, I redesigned the navigation and introduced filters to let students browse events and organizations in one place. I also improved the visual hierarchy by using clearer layouts, organization logos, and more readable descriptions to help students quickly find relevant information.

Beyond the desktop experience, I explored mobile layouts to make SoCalSocial more accessible across different screen sizes. I rethought how navigation, content, and key interactions would translate to smaller screens, focusing on making it easy for students to discover events, browse organizations, and manage their activities without overwhelming the interface.

Revisiting SoCalSocial taught me to think beyond individual screens and consider how different users interact with the same platform. I learned to balance the needs of students and organizations while improving navigation, visual hierarchy, and responsiveness across devices. It also showed me the value of revisiting earlier design decisions with a fresh perspective.

Takeaway

TEAM'S KEY INSIGHT

The problem isn't a lack of events. It's a lack of connection.

Building SoCalSocial taught my team and I that creating a product involves more than designing interfaces and implementing features. We had to consider the needs of both students and organizations while working within our technical abilities and project timeline.


Throughout the process, we learned to prioritize essential features, recognize potential risks early, and adapt when our initial ideas became too complex. Communication and collaboration helped us turn different ideas into one cohesive product.

MY LESSONS LEARNED

This project strengthened my skills in team management, collaboration, and problem-solving. I learned to coordinate responsibilities based on individual strengths, communicate design decisions, and keep the team aligned on a shared vision. Working across design and development challenged me to consider how design decisions affect implementation and the overall user experience.


Revisiting SoCalSocial independently gave me a new perspective on my original work. I became more intentional about information hierarchy, responsive design, and designing for different types of users.


Above all, I learned the importance of continuous evaluation, clear communication, and being willing to revisit earlier decisions.
