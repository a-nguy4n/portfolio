Overview

SoCalSocial is a campus event discovery and organization platform designed for UC San Diego students. The platform brings events from student organizations into one centralized space, making it easier for students to discover activities, save events they’re interested in, and keep track of what’s happening on campus.

Our team designed and developed SoCalSocial with both students and organizations in mind, creating an experience that simplifies event discovery for students while giving organizations a dedicated space to publish and manage their events.

Problem

THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Campus events are everywhere.
Finding them shouldn't be.

Campus events are shared across social media, flyers, club tabling, and word of mouth, making it difficult for students to discover what's happening and keep track of events they're interested in.

THE CHALLENGE

How might we create one centralized space for students to easily discover and organize campus events while giving student organizations a reliable way to publish and manage them?

Research

We used two research methods to understand both sides of the campus event landscape: how students discover and keep track of events and how student organizations promote events and reach their audiences.

01

User Interviews

We spoke with both students and members of student organizations to understand their experiences firsthand.

  • Students: How they discover events, keep track of them, and which platforms they already rely on.
  • Organizations: How they promote events, reach students, and manage information across different channels.
02

Desk Research

We examined the existing campus event landscape to understand where and how event information was being shared.

  • Students: Looked at the platforms and channels students encounter when searching for campus activities.
  • Organizations: Investigated how organizations use social media, messaging platforms, and other campus channels to promote their events.

STUDENT

Key Insights

  • Students discover campus events across multiple platforms and channels.
  • Finding the most relevant event details is not always quick or straightforward.
  • Students need an easier way to keep track of events they're interested in.

ORGANIZATION

Key Insights

  • Organizations often promote the same event across multiple platforms.
  • Organizations can struggle to reach students outside their existing audience.
  • Organizations need a central place to publish and maintain event information.
OUR DIRECTION

One shared space for both sides.

Our findings led us to create a shared platform where students could find and organize events, and organizations could post and manage them. With that direction established, we began defining the core experience needed to support both sides.

Design

Designing with Purpose

We explored how to bring SoCalSocial to life, from early wireframes to planning development. Along the way, we identified potential risks, worked through technical challenges, and made design decisions based on user needs and what we could realistically build.

01

FIRST DRAFT

Turning Requirements into Wireframes

We began by translating our MVP features into the screens students and organizations would need to complete their core tasks.

We started with low-fidelity sketches to explore how students would discover events and organizations, and how information would be organized across the platform. This allowed us to quickly visualize our ideas and discuss how the experience would differ between students and organizations before moving to more detailed wireframes.

WHAT WE LEARNED

We realized that events should be the central focus of the platform, connecting organizations that publish events with students who discover and manage them.

02

REFINING THE EXPERIENCE

Balancing Ideas with Feasibility

As we refined our designs, we considered the technical challenges of bringing our ideas to life. Features like calendars, social interactions, and account permissions added complexity, so we explored simpler alternatives that still addressed our users' needs.

    Design Decisions and Shifts

  • Full calendar → Organized event list
  • Broad social features → Event discovery
  • Complex permissions → Simpler accounts
  • Multiple interactions → Essential event actions

These decisions helped us keep the experience focused while reducing potential development challenges.

03

BUILDING IT

Planning Around Technical Challenges

Before development, we identified potential risks involving authentication, scalability, and code complexity. We considered how these challenges could affect both the user experience and our ability to build the platform within our timeline. Rather than attempting to implement every feature, we developed backup plans, such as simplifying account permissions and replacing the calendar with an organized event list if implementation proved too complex.

We also planned our technology stack around our team's existing experience, choosing React, TypeScript, Flask, and SQL. By dividing responsibilities based on our strengths, keeping the initial scope manageable, and considering limits on early users, we aimed to reduce development risks while leaving room for future improvements.

React TypeScript HTML / CSS Python Flask SQL
04

REFLECTING & REDESIGNING

Revisiting the Experience

After building SoCalSocial, I revisited the original designs to explore how I could improve the overall experience. I focused on making navigation more detailed and smooth, improving how event and organization information was presented, and adapting the interface for mobile devices.

For the Explore page, I redesigned the navigation and introduced filters to let students browse events and organizations in one place. I also improved the visual hierarchy by using clearer layouts, organization logos, and more readable descriptions to help students quickly find relevant information.

Beyond the desktop experience, I explored mobile layouts to make SoCalSocial more accessible across different screen sizes. I rethought how navigation, content, and key interactions would translate to smaller screens, focusing on making it easy for students to discover events, browse organizations, and manage their activities without overwhelming the interface.

Revisiting SoCalSocial taught me to think beyond individual screens and consider how different users interact with the same platform. I learned to balance the needs of students and organizations while improving navigation, visual hierarchy, and responsiveness across devices. It also showed me the value of revisiting earlier design decisions with a fresh perspective.

Takeaway

TEAM'S KEY INSIGHT

The problem isn't a lack of events.
It's a lack of connection.

Building SoCalSocial taught us that creating a product involves more than designing interfaces and implementing features. We had to consider the needs of both students and organizations while working within our technical abilities and project timeline. Throughout the process, we learned to prioritize essential features, recognize potential risks early, and adapt when our initial ideas became too complex. Communication and collaboration helped us turn different ideas into one cohesive product.

MY LESSONS LEARNED

This project strengthened my skills in team management, collaboration, and problem-solving. I learned to coordinate responsibilities based on individual strengths, communicate design decisions, and keep the team aligned on a shared vision. Working across design and development challenged me to consider how design decisions affect implementation and the overall user experience.

Revisiting SoCalSocial independently gave me a new perspective on my original work. I became more intentional about information hierarchy, responsive design, and designing for different types of users. Above all, I learned the importance of continuous evaluation, clear communication, and being willing to revisit earlier decisions.
